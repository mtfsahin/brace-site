Your coach for every set Brace Plus

Brace uses double progression, the method behind most proven programs: stay at a weight until you own the whole rep range, then go up. It reads your log and fills in today's targets for you.

Top of the range: add weight

When every working set reaches the top of its rep range, the next session adds the exercise's increment and starts again at the bottom of the range.

Not there yet: one more rep

Short of the top, the weight stays and the coach asks for one more rep than your best set last time.

Stalled twice: back off

Two sessions in a row below the bottom of the range bring the weight down by ten percent, so you can build back up with good reps.

The right step

Barbell lifts for the upper body go up 2.5 kg, for the legs 5 kg, dumbbells 2 kg. In pounds, 5 and 10 lb on a bar and 5 lb for the rest. Always a weight you can load.

Questions

How does the coach decide?

It looks at your last sessions of the same exercise, at the heaviest weight you used. If every working set reached the top of the rep range, it adds the exercise's step: 2.5 kg for upper-body barbell lifts, 5 kg for lower-body barbell lifts, 2 kg for dumbbells and kettlebells, 2.5 kg for machines and cables. If not, it keeps the weight and asks for one more rep. If two sessions in a row had a set below the bottom of the range, it takes the weight down ten percent to rebuild. The rep range comes from your routine, or 8 to 12 without one. Body-weight exercises add reps, timed holds add five seconds up to the top of the routine's range, and assisted ones take assistance away.

It is a starting point, not an order. Change any number before you lift, and the coach works from what you actually did.

Where does the readiness score come from?

From Apple Health: your heart rate variability and resting heart rate, which Apple Watch records, and last night's sleep. Brace compares last night with your own past four weeks, so a score of 50 means something for you, not for an average person. With no watch data, the card simply stays hidden. It is a training cue, not a health measurement.

Does the AI send my training anywhere?

No. The debrief, the routine builder and Ask Brace use the language model Apple runs on the iPhone itself, with Apple Intelligence. Brace hands it the facts its own calculations found, and the model only puts them into words. On iPhones without Apple Intelligence, those features are hidden and the debrief shows the same facts as plain text.

Kilograms or pounds?

Either. Brace starts with the unit your region uses, and you can switch in Settings at any time. Weights you logged keep their value when you switch; only how they show changes. In pounds, the coach steps up by 5 lb on upper-body barbell lifts, 10 lb on lower-body ones, and 5 lb on dumbbells, machines and cables.

Which bar and plates does it assume?

A 20 kg bar with 25, 20, 15, 10, 5, 2.5 and 1.25 kg plates, or a 45 lb bar with 45, 35, 25, 10, 5 and 2.5 lb plates. In Settings, pick a 15 or 10 kg bar (35 lb in pounds) or a custom one, and choose the plates your gym actually has, including 50 kg, 2 kg, 1 kg and 0.5 kg plates. The plate calculator and the warm-up sets use what you pick, and tell you when a weight can't be loaded exactly.

Do I need an Apple Watch?

No. Everything works on the iPhone alone, with the rest timer on the Lock Screen and in the Dynamic Island. The watch adds logging from your wrist and heart rate during the workout.

Can I bring my history from another app?

Yes, from Strong or Hevy, for free. Export your workouts in that app as a CSV file, then in Brace open History, tap the menu at the top and choose Import from Strong or Hevy. Strong's export doesn't say whether it used kilograms or pounds, so Brace asks. Workouts you already have are skipped, so importing twice is safe.

Is my training data private?

Yes. There is no account and no server: your log stays on your iPhone, your Apple Watch and your device backup. Brace saves workouts to Apple Health only if you turn that on, and reads your heart and sleep data only for the readiness score, once you allow it. We never see your data.

What is an estimated 1RM?

Your one-rep max is the most you could lift once. Brace estimates it from a set of up to 12 reps with the Epley formula: weight × (1 + reps ÷ 30). 80 kg for 10 reps estimates about 107 kg. It lets you compare a set of 5 with a set of 10 on one line, so you can see strength grow even in weeks without a new heaviest weight. It is an estimate for tracking, not a number to test.
